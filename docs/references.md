# References

## The literature designs

The program ships with 370 designs rebuilt from 58 published papers, in **File > Open sample… >
Literature**, grouped by category and paper. Each design's **Source** gives the full citation
and the table or figure its layers were taken from, and its **Notes** give what the paper
specifies and reports and how the design was checked against it. How they were checked, and
with what result, is in [verification.md](verification.md).

`thinfilm samples [words]` lists them from the command line, and the MCP server's
`list_samples` tool does the same.

## Methods

The calculation, and the optimisation methods the program uses:

- **The characteristic-matrix method** (Abelès matrices), by which the engine computes
  reflectance, transmittance, absorptance and phase. F. Abelès, "Recherches sur la propagation
  des ondes électromagnétiques sinusoïdales dans les milieux stratifiés. Application aux couches
  minces," Ann. Phys. (Paris) 12e série, 5, 596–640 and 706–782 (1950).
- **Needle optimisation.** A. V. Tikhonravov, M. K. Trubetskov, and G. W. DeBell, "Application of
  the needle optimization technique to the design of optical coatings," Appl. Opt. 35, 5493–5508
  (1996). B. T. Sullivan and J. A. Dobrowolski, "Implementation of a numerical needle method for
  thin-film design," Appl. Opt. 35, 5484–5492 (1996).
- **Gradual evolution.** A. V. Tikhonravov, M. K. Trubetskov, and G. W. DeBell, "Optical coating
  design approaches based on the needle optimization technique," Appl. Opt. 46, 704–710 (2007).
- **Damped least squares.** K. Levenberg, "A method for the solution of certain non-linear problems
  in least squares," Q. Appl. Math. 2, 164–168 (1944). D. W. Marquardt, "An algorithm for
  least-squares estimation of nonlinear parameters," J. Soc. Ind. Appl. Math. 11, 431–441 (1963).
- **Constrained optimisation: bounds by reflection.** A layer's minimum and maximum thickness are
  true bounds, not penalty rows in the merit function. A step that would carry a thickness past a
  bound is reflected back inside by the amount it overshot, not clamped to the bound: 620 nm
  against a 600 nm maximum becomes 580 nm. A clamped variable sits on its bound, contributing
  nothing while still taking part in the solve; a reflected one keeps moving. There are two
  exceptions. A floor of zero is not reflected, because zero is where a layer stops existing:
  needle and evolve drive a useless layer to zero and remove it. And a step that overshoots by
  more than the whole range is not folded back and forth; the solver takes a shorter step instead.
  How an optimiser treats a point outside its bounds has measurable consequences, and the three
  papers below study it. They study Differential Evolution, a stochastic method, so they motivate
  the choice rather than prove it for a least-squares solver:
  R. Boks, A. V. Kononova, and H. Wang, "Quantifying the impact of boundary constraint handling
  methods on Differential Evolution," GECCO '21 Companion, 1199–1207 (2021),
  [arXiv:2105.06757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06757). A. V. Kononova, F. Caraffini, and T. Bäck,
  "Differential evolution outside the box," Inf. Sci. 581, 587–604 (2021),
  [arXiv:2004.10489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10489). A. V. Kononova, D. Vermetten, F. Caraffini,
  M.-A. Mitran, and D. Zaharie, "The importance of being constrained: dealing with infeasible
  solutions in Differential Evolution and beyond," Evol. Comput. 32(1), 3–48 (2024),
  [arXiv:2203.03512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03512).
- **Basin hopping** (global search around the best design). D. J. Wales and J. P. K. Doye, "Global
  optimization by basin-hopping and the lowest energy structures of Lennard-Jones clusters
  containing up to 110 atoms," J. Phys. Chem. A 101, 5111–5116 (1997).
- **Thin-film optics.** H. A. Macleod, *Thin-Film Optical Filters*, 5th ed. (CRC Press, 2018).
  Sh. A. Furman and A. V. Tikhonravov, *Basics of Optics of Multilayer Systems* (Éditions
  Frontières, 1992).

## Material data

- **Metals, semiconductors and several dielectrics** (Ag, Al, Au, Cr, Cu, CuO, CdTe, CdSe, GaAs,
  Ge, InAs, InP, LiNbO3, MgF2 ordinary and extraordinary, MgO, Mo, Pd, Pt, Si3N4, SiO, TiN, Y2O3):
  measured tables from E. D. Palik, ed., *Handbook of Optical Constants of Solids* (Academic
  Press). How overlapping data sets were joined is described at the top of
  `materials/thinfilm-materials.txt`.
- **SiO2:** I. H. Malitson, "Interspecimen comparison of the refractive index of fused silica,"
  J. Opt. Soc. Am. 55, 1205–1209 (1965).
- **MgF2** (Sellmeier): M. J. Dodge, "Refractive properties of magnesium fluoride," Appl. Opt. 23,
  1980–1985 (1984).
- **Al2O3:** I. H. Malitson and M. J. Dodge, "Refractive index and birefringence of synthetic
  sapphire," J. Opt. Soc. Am. 62, 1405 (1972).
- **Nb2O5:** a sputtered film on BK7, from the refractiveindex.info database (M. N. Polyanskiy,
  "Refractiveindex.info database of optical constants," Sci. Data 11, 94 (2024)); method:
  J. Gao, F. Lemarchand, and M. Lequime, Opt. Express 20, 15734 (2012).
- **TiO2, Ta2O5, ZrO2, HfO2:** Cauchy coefficients whose origin the source file does not record;
  the material file says so.
- **Glass catalogues:** the SCHOTT, OHARA, HOYA, CDGM and SUMITA catalogues, in their published
  AGF format.
- **The materials of the literature designs** are each paper's own values, in
  `materials/literature-materials.txt`, every one with its source.

Every material in a design carries its own source, shown in **Edit > Materials**.

## Other data and standards

- **Solar spectrum** (operands ASOL, RSOL, TSOL): ASTM G173-03, *Standard Tables for Reference
  Solar Spectral Irradiances: Direct Normal and Hemispherical on 37° Tilted Surface* (ASTM
  International), global tilt spectrum.
- **Blackbody emission** (operand EMIT): Planck's law, at the temperature the operand gives.
